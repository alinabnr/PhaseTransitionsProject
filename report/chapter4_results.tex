\section{Results}

\subsection{Equilibrium Verification}

We verified equilibrium behavior by measuring $\langle E(T) \rangle$ and $\langle m(T) \rangle$ at our largest lattice size $L = 128$ after the burn-in. The curves reproduce the expected ferromagnetic ordering at low $T$ and disordered phase at high $T$, with susceptibility peaks near $T_c$. This validates our simulation settings used for the dynamical analysis.

\subsection{Autocorrelation Behavior}

\begin{figure}[h!]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width = 1 \textwidth]{../plots/tau_int_vs_beta_comparison_L128.png}
    \caption[Integrated autocorrelation functions for Metropolis and Wolff updates]{Integrated autocorrelation functions $\tau_{int,M}(t)$ of the magnetization for $L=128$ at criticality $\beta_c \approx 0.4407$, comparing Metropolis (blue) and Wolff (orange) updates.}
    \label{fig:AutocorrComparison}
\end{figure}

We illustrate the dynamical contrast between local and cluster updates in figure \ref{fig:AutocorrComparison} by plotting the integrated autocorrelation time of the energy versus $\beta$ at fixed $L=128$. 

For energy, Metropolis shows a pronounced peak centered near $\beta_c \approx 0.4407$ at $\tau_{int} = 206.97$, evidencing strong critical slowing down and long relaxation at the transition. By contrast, Wolff yields a small $\tau_{int} = 2.94$ at $\beta_c$, demonstrating that cluster updates suppress critical correlations and greatly reduce relaxation times. Thus, the Metropolis relaxation is slower by a factor of $\approx 70$.

For magnetization it is qualitatively the same. Metropolis shows a clear peak centered near $\beta_c \approx 0.4407$ at $\tau_{int} = 10948.72$. Conversely, Wolff yields $\tau_{int} = 1.59$ at $\beta_c$. Hence, the Metropolis relaxation is slower by a factor of $\approx 6.9 \times 10^3$.

This qualitative difference motivates our finite-size scaling of $\tau$ at $\beta_c$ to extract the dynamic exponent $z$.


\subsection{Finite-Size Scaling and Extraction of z}

For each lattice size $L$, we load the Monte Carlo time series of magnetization and energy at the critical inverse temperature $\beta_c = 0.4407$. 
We compute the normalized autocorrelation function using an FFT-based estimator, and determine the integrated autocorrelation time $\tau_{\mathrm{int}}$ using Sokal's self-consistent windowing procedure.
An iterative scheme is applied to automatically estimate and remove the thermalization (burn-in) period, ensuring that the remaining data satisfy $N \gg 50\,\tau_{\mathrm{int}}$. 
The resulting autocorrelation times for each $L$ are then fitted to the expected dynamic scaling form $\tau_{\mathrm{int}}(L) \sim L^z$ in log-log scale.
In this scale, we can fit a straight line to the data points with the slope representing the dynamic critical exponent $z$.
This allows us to extract the dynamic critical exponent $z$ for both the magnetization and the energy via the slope. These plots can be seen in figures \ref{fig:FSSMetropolis} and \ref{fig:FSSWolff}.

\begin{figure}[h!]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width = 0.55 \textwidth]{../plots/metropolis_fss_tau_vs_L.png}
    \caption[Finite-size scaling of integrated autocorrelation times for Metropolis updates]{Finite-size scaling of integrated autocorrelation time $\tau$ versus system size $L$ at criticality $\beta_c \approx 0.4407$ for energy (blue) and magnetization (orange) using Metropolis updates.}
    \label{fig:FSSMetropolis}
\end{figure}

\begin{figure}[h!]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width = 0.6 \textwidth]{../plots/wolff_fss_tau_vs_L.png}
    \caption[Finite-size scaling of integrated autocorrelation times for Wolff updates]{Finite-size scaling of integrated autocorrelation time $\tau$ versus system size $L$ at criticality $\beta_c \approx 0.4407$ for energy (blue) and magnetization (orange) using Wolff updates.}
    \label{fig:FSSWolff}
\end{figure}

The extracted dynamic exponents show a stark algorithmic contrast. For Metropolis we find $z_M \approx 2.03 \pm 0.03$ (magnetization) and $z_E \approx 1.39 \pm 0.06$ (energy). The magnetization result is close to the expected $z \approx 2.1$ (\cite{Adzhemyan2022}) for local dynamics, while the energy-based estimate is noticeably lower, reflecting that $\tau_E$ is harder to measure reliably, because energy time series have smaller signal-to-noise, broader multi-time-scale structure, and stronger finite-size/systematic effects near $\beta_c$, whereas magnetization directly probes the slow order-parameter mode that controls critical relaxation.
For Wolff, $z_M \approx 0.217 \pm 0.035$ and $z_E \approx 0.367 \pm 0.021$ both indicate a dramatic suppression of critical slowing down. The magnetization-based value lies within the expected $0 - 0.3$ range, while the energy-based value is somewhat higher, again consistent with noisier $\tau_E$ estimates and the fact that cluster flips most efficiently decorrelate the order parameter, leaving residual shorter scales in energy. Overall, $M$ provides the more reliable estimator of z for both algorithms, with E tending to bias low for Metropolis and high for Wolff due to noise and mixed time scales.
