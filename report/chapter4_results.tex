\section{Results}

\subsection{Equilibrium Verification}

We verified equilibrium behavior by measuring $\langle E(T) \rangle$ and $\langle m(T) \rangle$ at our largest lattice size $L = 128$. The curves reproduce the expected ferromagnetic ordering at low $T$ and disordered phase at high $T$, with susceptibility peaks near $T_c$. This validates our simulation settings used for the dynamical analysis.

    \caption[Energy per spin versus $\beta$ using Metropolis updates]{Energy per spin versus $\frac{\langle E \rangle}{N}$ inverse temperature $\beta$ for the 2D Ising model using Metropolis updates. Points show the equilibrium average energy.}
    \label{fig:MetropolisEvsBeta}
\end{figure}

\begin{figure}[h!]
    \centering

    \caption[Energy per spin versus $\beta$ using Wolff updates]{Energy per spin versus $\frac{\langle E \rangle}{N}$ inverse temperature $\beta$ for the 2D Ising model using Wolff updates. Points show the equilibrium average energy.}
    \label{fig:WolffEvsBeta}
\end{figure}

In figures \ref{fig:MetropolisEvsBeta} and \ref{fig:WolffEvsBeta} we can see that for both algorithms, as $\beta$ increases, $\frac{\langle E \rangle}{N}$ decreases smoothly and exhibits a pronounced change in slope near the critical $\beta_c \approx 0.4407$ (vertical reference line).

Magnetization plots in figures \ref{fig:MetropolisMvsBeta} and \ref{fig:WolffMvsBeta} show the expected spontaneous magnetization below $T_c$ and decay to zero above $T_c$ for both algorithms, confirming correct equilibrium sampling.

\begin{figure}[h!]

    \label{fig:WolffSuscVsBeta}
\end{figure}

Susceptibility plots in figures \ref{fig:MetropolisSuscVsBeta} and \ref{fig:WolffSuscVsBeta} exhibit clear peaks near $T_c$ for both algorithms, indicating critical fluctuations as expected.
It is to be noted that we plotted the susceptibility computed from the absolute magnetization $|M|$ rather than $M$ itself, which prevents sector cancellations, as due to $\mathbb{Z}_2$ symmetry finite systems flip between magnetized sectors. This yields the standard finite-size peak near $\beta_c$.
That choice does not affect our determination of $T_c$ or the scaling analysis.


\subsection{Autocorrelation Behavior}

\begin{figure}[h!]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width = 1 \textwidth]{../plots/tau_int_vs_beta_comparison_L128.png}
    \caption[Integrated autocorrelation functions for Metropolis and Wolff updates]{Integrated autocorrelation functions $\rho_M(t)$ of the magnetization for $L=128$ at criticality $\beta_c \approx 0.4407$, comparing Metropolis (blue) and Wolff (orange) updates.}
    \label{fig:AutocorrComparison}
\end{figure}

We illustrate the dynamical contrast between local and cluster updates in figure \ref{fig:AutocorrComparison} by plotting the integrated autocorrelation time of the energy versus $\beta$ at fixed $L=128$. 

For energy, Metropolis shows a pronounced peak centered near $\beta_c \approx 0.4407$ at $\tau_{int} = 206.97$, evidencing strong critical slowing down and long relaxation at the transition. By contrast, Wolff yields a small $\tau_{int} = 2.94$ at $\beta_c$, demonstrating that cluster updates suppress critical correlations and greatly reduce relaxation times. Thus, the Metropolis relaxation is slower by a factor of $\approx 70$.

For magnetization it is qualitatively the same. Metropolis shows a clear peak centered near $\beta_c \approx 0.4407$ at $\tau_{int} = 10948.72$. Conversely, Wolff yields $\tau_{int} = 1.59$ at $\beta_c$. Hence, the Metropolis relaxation is slower by a factor of $\approx 6.9 \times 10^3$.

This qualitative difference motivates our finite-size scaling of $\tau$ at $\beta_c$ to extract the dynamic exponent $z$.
